# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Static HTML, CSS and JavaScript in one file (`app/index.html`). No framework, no build. Responsive web app, used on laptop and phone alike.

## Users

One person, the owner, managing their own life. Right now that includes planning a wedding and a job hunt alongside everyday errands.

## Product Purpose

A personal to-do list that is pleasant enough to open many times a day. Success is that the owner actually keeps using it: things get captured the moment they come up, and big life projects stay organised without the tool feeling like work.

## Positioning

A tool, not a system. It does a few things and does them with charm: loose tasks and colour-coded groups for the big things, one due date per task, a calendar of what is due. It deliberately avoids the productivity-app machinery of priorities, labels, time blocks, streaks and accounts.

## Operating Context

- Quick capture, many times a day, on whichever device is at hand.
- Sitting down to work through one big project, such as the wedding or the job hunt.
- The first thing to see on opening is today: what is due and what is late.
- Used on laptops and phones of any make; the layout must be genuinely good at both sizes.
- Dark theme at all times.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Tasks: add, complete with undo, edit, delete. Each has an optional note and an optional due date.
- Groups: named, coloured; a task is in one group or none.
- Calendar: month view of due dates, coloured by group.
- Done list: collapsible, clearable.
- Storage: in the browser only. No accounts, no server. Backup and restore through a JSON file, with a reminder when a backup is overdue.
- Hosting at a web address is still open; needed for phone use.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: none yet; working title "To-do".
- Dark theme only.
- Feel: cute and fun, something the owner looks forward to opening. Two earlier looks were rejected: a plain dark list ("very basic") and solid colour-slab cards ("generic, not cute or fun enough").

## Evidence on Hand

No real user data, testimonials or assets. Example content in demos must be illustrative, not presented as the owner's real tasks.

## Product Principles

1. Capture beats organisation: adding a task must always be one step away.
2. Today first; everything else is one tap away.
3. Big things get their own space, small things stay loose.
4. Charm lives in the moments that repeat (adding, finishing), and never slows them down.
5. Never lose the owner's data silently.
